# The dates the site talked about

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Status:** design, SECOND draft. Review found one blocking defect and it was
the number three decisions rested on; §9 records it.
**Scope:** frontend only. The field has been on the wire since the report
generator shipped; nothing backend changes.

---

## 1. What this is

`critical_dates_and_deadlines` is generated by `lambda_report_generator` and
passed straight through by `render_report_shape`
(`lambda_org_api.py:5911`). **Nothing in `scripts/` has ever read it** — a
repo-wide grep outside `scripts/mock/` returns zero. The fixture carries it,
which is its own small irony: the mock teaches a contract the app ignores.

It is the fourth field found this way, after the sort key's time-of-day,
findings' severity/domain, and the thread's date span
(`2026-09-07-payload-fields-the-ui-never-reads.md`). This one is the largest.

## 2. Measured, on the whole prod corpus

| entries | **239** — median **3** per report, max **12** |

By type: deadline 107 · meeting 52 · other 41 · delivery 15 · inspection 15 ·

By urgency: **high 115** · medium 93 · low 31.

Verbatim, unedited:

- *"Carpet installation to be ready in Building 2"* — Wednesday (2026-02-11), high
- *"3 floor boxes ready for installation in Building 2"* — Tomorrow morning, high
- *"Barbecue scheduled on site, Richard organizing"* — Wednesday, low

Each entry is `{date_mentioned, context, who_mentioned, urgency, type}`.

## 3. Three things the numbers settle

**It is not a duplicate of the to-do list.** 6 % overlap, the same proportion
findings had. The rest is commitments and events nobody turned into a task.

**`urgency` cannot rank it.** 115 of 239 are `high` — 48 %, the identical
proportion that made `priority` unusable as a sort key for Today's open items
(169 of 349) and that this repo has now been burned by twice. It is a badge.
Whatever orders this list, it is not urgency.

**Most of it CAN be ordered, and the first draft said the opposite.**
`FS.api.resolveDeadline` (`today-adapter.js:203`) resolves weekday names,
today/tomorrow, "next week" and "within N days" against the report's own date.
Driven over all 239 entries it resolves **184 (77 %)** — not the 26 % the first
draft claimed, which counted ISO literals in the lake rather than what this
frontend can do. Two of that draft's own "unresolvable" examples resolve:

    Wednesday (2026-02-11)   -> 2026-02-11
    Tomorrow morning         -> 2026-02-10
    Wednesday                -> 2026-02-11
    Next week                -> 2026-02-16
    This afternoon           -> null

The 55 that do not resolve are genuinely unorderable: `"This afternoon"` (6),
`"End of week"`, `"3 months"`, `"October"`, `"21st"`.

**It is still not a deadline list.** 48 % are `high`, 22 % are meetings and 4 %
are weather. What the resolver buys is an ORDER, not a promise that these are
commitments. The section stays **"dates the site talked about"**.

**Median 3, max 12** is what makes this a small section inside a day rather
than a page. Nothing here justifies a new nav entry.

## 4. Where it goes: the Timeline day, not Today

**Today is for open items** — things with an owner and a state. A date somebody
mentioned is neither. Putting it there would re-expand the page its owner has
recently and deliberately made smaller (the activity feed was cut outright and
a larger restructure was rejected).

**The Timeline day view is where a day's context already lives** — its
summary, its topics, and since 2026-09-07 its photos. This is one more fact
about that day, next to the others, and it inherits the day's own scoping and
permissions for free.

Placement: a section after the topics, before Ask — the same slot the day's
photos took, and for the same reason.

```
Dates mentioned - 4

  Wed 11 Feb    Carpet installation to be ready in Building 2      HIGH
  Tue 10 Feb    3 floor boxes ready for installation, Building 2   HIGH
  Thu 12 Feb    Gloves left behind after Thursday meeting
  This afternoon    Racking delivery window                        HIGH
```

**Both columns come from the resolver, and that is a change from the first
draft's mockup.** `resolveDeadline` returns `{absolute, display}`, and for a
relative phrase `display` is the RESOLVED day, not the words: 169 of the 239
entries have a `display` that differs from what was said. So a row reading
"Tomorrow" in the first draft actually renders as the date. That is the right
outcome for a list someone plans from — "Tomorrow" printed under a day header
three weeks later is a trap — and the original wording survives in `context`.

The 55 unresolvable entries show `date_mentioned` verbatim, because there is
nothing else to show and the phrase is the only thing the reader has.

**The site-aggregated day needs its own mount, or it gets nothing.**
`AggregatedDayView` (`timeline.js:920`) renders one section per person from
`section.report` and has NO photo slot and no Ask — "the same slot the photos
took" exists only in the single-user view. This is the two-mount rule the file
already documents for TopicCard. v1 ships the single-user mount; the aggregated
one is a follow-up, and saying so is better than a section that silently is not
there.

## 5. Rules

**Order: resolved dates first, ascending; then the rest in payload order.**
Resolved means `resolveDeadline(date_mentioned, report_date).absolute` is
non-null - 184 of 239, not the 63 the first draft assumed. The remaining 55
keep the order the extractor gave them.

**Reuse `resolveDeadline`; do not write a second resolver.** The first draft
proposed ordering only on ISO literals, which is precisely the split
`today-ordering.js:84-92` already rejected in writing: *"An earlier draft tried
to separate 'a real date' from 'a parsed guess' and sort only the former. That
distinction does not exist here."* Doing it here would contradict the rule this
spec claims to be copying, in the same repo, three weeks later.

**`"Week of 2026-07-23 or 2026-07-27"` resolves to the FIRST date**, silently.
That is the resolver's existing behaviour and this spec accepts it rather than
special-casing: one entry in the corpus, and the full phrase stays visible in
`context`. Written down because a reader who spots it should find it decided,
not overlooked.

**`urgency` is a chip on `high` only.** `medium` is 39 % of the corpus and a
chip on it would mark two rows in five, which marks nothing. `low` likewise
says "do not look here", which a chip cannot say quietly.

**`type` is not rendered in v1.** Six values, and the useful distinction
(deadline vs meeting vs weather) is already legible from the text. Adding six
chip colours to a four-row list is decoration.

**`who_mentioned` renders only when it is not the report's own author.** On a
single-author day it is the same name on every row, which is noise.

**The section does not render when the field is absent or empty.** An older
report, or a day nobody named a date on. `0 dates mentioned` is a sentence
about a section that should not exist.

## 6. What this deliberately does not do

**No cross-day rollup.** "Everything due next week across all sites" is a
different feature with a different question behind it, and it would have to
decide what to do with the 23 % that resolve to nothing before it could
promise "everything".

**No link to action items.** The 6 % that overlap are not marked as such.
Matching free text to a to-do is the same class of problem as the
finding→programme matcher, which took a model and a candidate gate — and that
one has produced zero links on prod for want of input.

**No editing.** These are read-time passthrough from the report document, not
Aurora rows; there is nothing to PATCH.

## 7. Testing

- **Ordering is a pure function.** Assert: resolvable dates ascending;
  unresolvable ones after them in payload order; nothing dropped (input length
  equals output length); a list of entirely unresolvable dates keeps its
  original order exactly.
- **The `high`-only chip.** Feed one of each urgency and assert exactly one
  chip. This is the assertion that stops a later "let's show medium too".
- **Absent and empty both render nothing** — not an empty section, not a zero.
- **Real prod entries, with expected outputs.** Against report date
  2026-02-09, verified by running the real resolver:

  | date_mentioned | absolute |
  |---|---|
  | `Wednesday (2026-02-11)` | `2026-02-11` |
  | `Tomorrow morning` | `2026-02-10` |
  | `Wednesday` | `2026-02-11` |
  | `Next week` | `2026-02-16` |
  | `Week of 2026-07-23 or 2026-07-27` | `2026-07-23` |
  | `This afternoon` | `null` |
  | `End of week` / `3 months` / `21st` | `null` |

  The fixture is not allowed to be the only input this is exercised on - the
  last three features in this repo each had a defect that only real payload
  shapes exposed.
- **The fixture's `type` is wrong and a test must not encode it.**
  `daily-report.fixture.js:482` uses `milestone`; the generator's enum
  (`lambda_report_generator.py:179`) is
  `deadline|inspection|delivery|weather|meeting|other` and prod never emits
  `milestone`. Fix the fixture in the same change. v1 renders no `type`, so
  nothing breaks either way - but a mock teaching a value the backend cannot
  send is how the next feature gets built against a shape that does not
  exist.
- **Browser:** the section renders on a day that has entries, does not on a day
  that does not, the count equals the rows, and both themes are legible.

## 8. Answered, not open

The first draft deferred *"is the day view per-report or aggregated?"* to
implementation time. It is answerable from the code and the answer is
**per report**: `TimelineMiddleColumn` (`timeline.js:1375`) renders one
`getTimeline` result for one folder; the site-wide route
(`timeline.js:1756-1759`) hands off to `AggregatedDayView`, which renders one
section per person off `section.report`.

So `who_mentioned` is compared against `report.user_name` in the single view
and `section.report.user_name` in the aggregated one, and no per-row author is
needed. Deferring this bought nothing - the two greps that answer it are the
same two an implementer would have had to run anyway.

## 9. What the first draft got wrong

One defect, and it was the number three decisions rested on.

| claim | reality |
|---|---|
| "only 26 % carry a date anything could resolve" | `resolveDeadline` resolves **184 of 239 (77 %)**. The 26 % counted ISO literals in the lake, not what this frontend can do. |
| `"Wednesday"` and `"Tomorrow morning"` are unresolvable | both resolve, against the report's own date. They were the draft's own examples. |
| ordering only ISO dates "is the rule the Today sort already uses" | inverted - `today-ordering.js:84-92` explicitly rejects that split, in writing, in this repo. |
| the mockup rendering `Tomorrow` / `Wednesday` verbatim | `display` is the resolved day for 169 of 239 entries; those rows render as dates. |
| "the same slot the photos took" | that slot exists only in the single-user view; `AggregatedDayView` has neither a photo mount nor Ask. |

The pattern is the one this repo keeps producing: **measured the lake, designed
for the page.** The Evidence spec made the identical mistake ten hours earlier
and was corrected the same way. The check that catches it costs one command -
run the real consumer over the real strings before quoting a percentage.
